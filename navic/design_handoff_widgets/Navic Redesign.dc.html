Navic · design polish study

A more consistent, more expressive Navic

Two polished directions on top, the current build at the bottom for reference. Focus screens: Library, Albums & album detail, and Search. Everything below is the real Material 3 baseline, Google Sans, and Navic's own iconography — restructured for rhythm and consistency. Reference any frame by its badge (e.g. 1a) in chat.

1a

Direction A — “Editorial”

recommended

A single, warm amber theme replaces the default purple. One consistent section pattern everywhere — an uppercase overline + count, a unified card, one cover-radius (12dp) across the whole app. Bigger display type sets hierarchy; the accent does the pointing. This is the fix for “does it feel consistent.”

1b

Direction B — “Ambient”

alternate, higher-motion

Leans harder into the cover-colour ambient wash Navic already has on the album page — but makes it the whole app's identity, not just one screen. Chrome dematerialises into frosted floating pills; the cover art does the theming. More alive, more distinctive, a bigger engineering lift than 1a.

Rebuilt the wash — this was too close to the current build
A flat hue-locked gradient still read as "the same screen, recoloured." So the background is no longer a gradient swatch at all — it's the cover art itself, blown up and heavily blurred behind a darkening scrim (the real Apple-Music/Spotify technique), so it carries the photo's actual colour and texture, not an approximation of its hue. The sharp cover then floats as a small card on top. That's a genuinely different composition from today's edge-to-edge photo + flat fade below it — not just a different palette.
Home / Library — keyed to the now-playing track, not the whole grid
Library shows a dozen different album covers on screen at once, so washing the entire page in one colour would fight with all of them. Instead: the glow comes from whatever's in the mini-player (the one thing that's actually "now"), sits only behind the greeting/header — like Spotify's home hero — and resolves to the neutral surface by the time you reach the grids, so every cover underneath still reads true. The floating mini-player itself keeps a persistent ring of that colour, so the identity carries even after the glow fades out. Nothing playing → no glow, just the plain dark surface (below, in 1a's palette) — it never fakes a colour that isn't there.
